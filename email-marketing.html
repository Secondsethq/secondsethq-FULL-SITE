The AI question

AI writes emails. It doesn't send them.

Email is the place where AI looks most convincing and helps least, because the writing was never the hard part. Deciding who gets what, and when, is.

What AI genuinely does well

  • Drafts a decent first version far faster than you would
  • Rewrites something long into something readable
  • Generates subject line options worth testing
  • Summarizes what a customer asked across a long thread
  • Translates your notes into a usable outline

What it cannot do on its own

  • Decide who should receive it and who should not
  • Fire at the right moment, then stop when someone replies
  • Keep your tags and segments clean as contacts move around
  • Notice that a sequence has been sending to the wrong list for a month
  • Tell you whether any of it produced revenue

Everyone can write an email now. Almost nobody has a system that sends the right one.

Why the plumbing matters more than the prose

One practical example. Most platforms cannot reliably detect that a customer replied to you directly, so a sequence will happily keep chasing someone who already said yes. That is a systems problem, and it is solved with tagging and exit conditions, not with a better prompt.

Proof

A funnel that knows when to stop.

For Alchemy Print Co. we built a ten-email funnel across three journeys: warm customers, cold outreach, and inbound quote follow-up. The interesting part was not the writing.

The constraint
  • The platform could not detect when a customer replied directly to an email.
  • Built-in exit conditions required a connected online store, which a print shop does not have.
  • Left alone, that means a customer who has already said yes keeps getting chased for a week.
  • That is worse than no follow-up at all, because it makes an attentive business look careless.
How we solved it
  • If/else gates before every email, checking for a "replied" tag rather than trusting the platform.
  • Form submissions tagged automatically through Zapier, in an account the client already owned.
  • Three separate journeys so warm, cold, and inbound contacts never receive the wrong message.
  • A written handover making clear which single manual step keeps the whole thing honest.

We flagged the manual step in writing rather than hiding it. Every automation has one weak joint, and the client knowing exactly where it is beats a system that looks seamless right up until it embarrasses them.

Questions

The things people actually ask.

Does email marketing still work?
Yes, and it is still the highest-returning channel most small businesses have access to. The catch is that it only works when the sequences are actually built and running, not when a newsletter goes out whenever somebody remembers. A list nobody emails is not an asset.
How often should I email my list?
For most local businesses, once or twice a month for broadcasts, plus automated sequences that fire on their own. Frequency matters less than relevance. People unsubscribe because the emails are not useful to them, not because they arrived too often.
What is the difference between a broadcast and an automation?
A broadcast goes to everyone at once, like a seasonal offer. An automation fires for one person based on what they did, like a welcome series or a quote follow-up. Automations do most of the earning because they reach people at the moment they care.
Can I just use AI to write my emails?
For a first draft, often yes. What AI cannot do is know who should receive it, when it should send, when it should stop because someone replied, or whether it worked. Those are the parts that decide whether an email list makes money.
Which platform should I use?
Usually whichever you already pay for. Mailchimp, Klaviyo, Zoho, and Constant Contact all do the job for a small business. We build in the one you own rather than moving you, unless your platform genuinely cannot do what you need it to do.
What if my list is old or I bought it?
We will not send to a purchased or scraped list. Mailchimp and most platforms explicitly prohibit it, and it wrecks your sending reputation for years. An old list of real customers is different, and can usually be warmed back up carefully.
Will people unsubscribe?
Some will, and that is fine. An unsubscribe is someone removing themselves from a list they were never going to buy from. What matters is the trend in replies and revenue, not list size. We would rather have 400 people who read than 4,000 who do not.
Do I need to write the emails myself?
No, we write them and it is included in every package. You will need to tell us what you actually sell and the questions customers ask most, which takes about an hour on the first call. Everything after that is ours until you approve it.
How do I know if it is working?
By revenue attributed to email, plus replies and bookings, not by open rates. Apple Mail Privacy Protection has made open rates unreliable since 2021, so anyone still reporting them as the headline number is measuring something that stopped meaning much years ago.
