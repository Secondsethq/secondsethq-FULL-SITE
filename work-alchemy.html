Screen Printing · Landing Pages & Funnel

Alchemy Print Co.

Four audience-specific landing pages, lead capture, analytics, and an automated email follow-up funnel — a complete lead-generation system for a screen printing shop in Vista, CA, plus a local SEO rebuild and a cleanup of every platform the business appears on.

The brief

Alchemy Print Co. is a screen printing shop that serves very different customers — schools ordering spirit wear, businesses placing bulk orders, event planners booking live on-site printing, and small brands doing apparel runs. One generic "get a quote" page was asking all of them the same questions and converting none of them well.

The fix was a set of dedicated landing pages, one per audience, each speaking directly to that customer's job and asking only the questions that matter to them — then feeding every lead into one place, with tracking to prove what's working and automated follow-up so no quote request goes cold.

Everything was built to be owned and cheap to run: static pages for fast, reliable hosting, a single lead-capture platform, and automation that lives in tools Adam already pays for.

Before we built anything

We audited what was there.

Adam was already paying for ads. The problem was not traffic, it was that the pages the traffic landed on could not convert and could not be found. We scored the site against our Local SEO Standard before writing a single line of new copy.

What we found
  • The homepage title read "Custom Printed Clothes & Apparel." The H1 read "Custom T-Shirts & Apparel Designed By You." Neither contained "screen printing," which is the service, or "Vista," which is the city.
  • "Let's Explore," "Call us today," "Schedule a Booking," and "Consult with an expert" were all dead links.
  • The phone link was malformed and would not dial on a mobile.
  • No inquiry form existed anywhere on the page.
  • A section on the homepage still read "Sub-text here" where copy had never been written.
  • The live event page used the phrase "live screen printing" zero times. The bulk page used its own target phrase exactly once.
What we changed
  • Titles and H1s that name the service and the city, because that is what people actually type into a search box.
  • A working quote form on every page, wired to conversion tracking rather than pageviews.
  • Every call to action pointing somewhere real, and a phone link that dials on the first tap.
  • Copy written at a keyword density that reads naturally and still makes a claim on the term.
  • Individual city names in visible copy, since someone in Encinitas searches "screen printing Encinitas," never "screen printing San Diego County."
  • No placeholder text anywhere. Every page finished before it went live.

This engagement is where our Local SEO Standard came from. It is a six-category scoring framework weighted for businesses that get a few hundred visitors a month rather than a few hundred thousand, and we now run it for every client before quoting work.

Structured data

Built to be read by machines too.

Almost no small business site carries structured data, which makes it unusually cheap advantage. It is also the main mechanism by which AI search engines understand a business well enough to name it in an answer. Every page carries a full set, and it took under an hour per page.

  • LocalBusiness with address, phone, geo coordinates, and an 18-city areaServed covering the real service radius
  • Service markup describing what is actually sold on each of the four pages
  • FAQPage matching every question visible in the on-page accordion, because mismatched FAQ schema is a manual-action risk rather than a benefit
  • WebPage with breadcrumbs, so the site hierarchy is legible
  • openingHoursSpecification and memberOf for the Chamber of Commerce

The honest framing: rewriting website copy for AI is close to the least effective lever a local business has, because roughly 85 percent of brand mentions in AI answers come from third-party pages rather than the brand's own site. The work that moves the needle is the local proof below.

Online presence

One business, one set of facts.

Google cross-references a business name, address, and phone number across the web to decide how confident it is that the business is real and where it is. Alchemy's details disagreed with themselves in four places. We opened every listing on page one of a branded search, wrote down what each one said, and made them match down to the punctuation.
