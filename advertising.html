The usual state of things

Most ad accounts leak quietly.

Nobody is doing anything dramatic wrong. Small problems just stack up when no one is looking at the account every week.

What we usually find
  • One monthly payment, with no way to see how much of it reached Google or Meta
  • An ad account that belongs to the agency, so the history leaves when you do
  • Tracking that counts page views as leads, or no tracking at all
  • Search ads showing for searches that have nothing to do with the business
  • The same ad running for a year after people stopped noticing it
  • Reports full of impressions and clicks, with no calls or forms anywhere
What you get instead
  • Our fee and your ad spend on separate bills, so the split is never a question
  • Accounts in your name, with you as the owner and us as a user
  • Tracking on the calls and forms that actually come in
  • Search terms reviewed weekly, with the wasted ones blocked
  • New ads before the old ones go stale, written and designed in house
  • Reporting on cost per lead, the number that decides whether ads are worth it
The big-platform question

Bigger doesn't buy cheaper clicks.

Large ad resellers sometimes say their size gets them better pricing on ads. Google and Meta both price ads through an auction, based on your bid and how relevant your ad is. A reseller enters the same auction you would. What size buys them is software and reporting, not wholesale clicks.

What a bundled package gives you

  • One bill that covers everything
  • A dashboard and a login
  • A sales rep and an account team
  • Extras like call tracking, sometimes included

Questions to ask before you sign

  • How much of my monthly payment reaches Google or Meta, in writing?
  • Who owns the ad account if I leave?
  • Can I see the platform's own numbers, not only your dashboard?
  • Is there a contract term or a cancellation fee?
  • Who actually works on my account, and how many others do they handle?

If someone won't tell you how much of your budget reaches Google, that is the answer.

Questions

The things people actually ask.

How much should a small business spend on ads?
For most local businesses, at least $500 a month per platform is where results become readable. The right number depends on what a customer is worth to you. If one booked job pays for a month of ads, you can spend more. We work that out with you before anything goes live.
Why a flat fee instead of a percentage of spend?
A percentage fee pays the agency more when you spend more, whether or not the extra spend is working. A flat fee means our pay doesn't change with your budget, so we can tell you to spend less when that is the right call.
Do I pay you for the ads themselves?
No. Google and Meta bill your own card directly. Our fee is a separate bill for the work. That way you always know exactly how much reached the platforms, because you can see it on your own statement.
Who owns the ad accounts?
You do. Accounts are set up in your business name with you as the owner, and we are added as users. If you ever stop working with us, you remove our access and keep everything, including the history.
Which platforms do you run?
Google Ads, and Meta, which covers Facebook and Instagram from one account. We can also run Yelp ads. We will tell you on the first call which one makes sense first for your business, and it is often only one.
What does setup or cleanup include?
Accounts moved into or created in your name, conversion tracking on calls and forms, campaigns built new or rebuilt from what you already have, and wasted spend switched off. If your current account is a bigger job than usual, the audit shows that before we quote.
How soon will I see results?
Search ads can bring calls within the first week or two. Facebook and Instagram usually take a few weeks to settle as the platform learns who responds. Give any new account about three months before deciding whether ads are worth it for your business.
Can you teach me to run my own ads?
Yes. We can clean up your account and walk you through running it yourself, billed at $85 an hour. Some owners start with us managing it and take it over later, which is easy because the accounts are already theirs.
What does advertising management cost?
$400 a month for one platform plus a $500 one-time setup, or $650 a month for Google and Meta together plus an $850 one-time setup. Ad spend is separate and paid directly to the platforms. One-off reviews, fixes and training are $85 an hour.
