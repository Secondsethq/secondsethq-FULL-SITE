Timber Framing & Fine Carpentry · Mineral Wells, TX

DC Signature Woodworks

A brand and a website for a North Texas timber framing and carpentry company. Within two weeks of going live it had brought in roughly $70,000 in signed contracts and put two residential developers in direct contact with the shop.

The brief

DC Signature Woodworks builds heavy timber structures, entry gates, pavilions and pergolas, custom decks and barndominium build-outs out of Mineral Wells, working across Parker and Palo Pinto counties and into the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex. It is skilled, specialised work, and the business was almost entirely dependent on word of mouth to find it.

The job was to give the shop an identity and a website that matched the quality of the joinery, and that could be found and understood by someone planning a build rather than someone who already knew the name.

Branding · Websites · Print & Packaging
Identity

A mark made of the material.

The logo is a log read end on. The D is the face of the timber with its grain and growth rings drawn in, and the C is cut out of it in char. It works at the size of a favicon and at the size of a truck door, which matters for a business whose signage is a job site.

Six colors taken off the material itself, each with a job. Chivo carries the type in three weights. Every rule is written down, which is the part that keeps a brand consistent once other people start using it.

The brand sheet

One page he can hand to a sign writer, a printer or anyone else who needs to use the mark. Color, type, logo variants, clear space, minimum sizes, and a short list of what not to do.

Print and pocket

Something to hand over, something to send.

A builder meets people on site, so the brand had to work on paper as well as on a screen. Cards for each member of the team, and a digital contact card behind a QR code so a client can save the number without typing it.

The digital card

  • Call, text and email in one tap, so nobody has to copy a number off a photo
  • Save to contacts writes the whole card into a phone's address book
  • A QR code on the printed card opens the same page
  • One per person, so the card belongs to whoever handed it over

Email signature

The same identity on every message that leaves the business.

The new site.

Dark, heavy and quiet, to match the material. A separate page for each trade, so someone searching for a timber gate lands on timber gates rather than a homepage.
